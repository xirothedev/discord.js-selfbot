## Account risk

A selfbot is automation on a user account. Discord&apos;s Terms of Service prohibit this. Discord can rate-limit, suspend, or terminate the account.

- Assume the account can be lost.
- Do not use an account that owns servers, payment methods, or Nitro credit.
- Do not run destructive automation against a community you do not control.
- Do not use a selfbot to harass, scrape, or evade moderation.

## Token handling

The token is a password. Store it in an environment variable or a secret manager. Never commit it. If it leaks, change the password and log out all sessions.

Read [Token safety](/docs/getting-started/token-safety) for the full checklist.

## Stubbed relationship mutations

These methods exist in the typings but reject without a request:

- `RelationshipManager#addFriend`
- `RelationshipManager#deleteRelationship`
- `RelationshipManager#sendFriendRequest`
- `RelationshipManager#addBlocked`

They are marked `@deprecated` and `@experimental`. They are not implemented because automated relationship changes can cause account harm and are hard to test safely. A separate safety review is required before they ship.

## Voice and recording

- Voice and video recording can capture other users without their knowledge. Check the law and the community rules in your jurisdiction.
- Recording consumes CPU, bandwidth, and disk. Stop the recorder before you disconnect.
- The voice engine is tested at the payload boundary. It does not guarantee every codec or container combination works.

## Destructive guild methods

`Guild#delete`, `Guild#leave`, `Guild#setOwner`, and the bulk channel/role position methods change public state. Add a confirmation step and test on a small guild first.

## Reporting a security issue

Do not open a public issue for a token leak, a remote code execution path, or a vulnerability that could harm users. Contact the maintainer through the repository&apos;s private security advisory flow.
